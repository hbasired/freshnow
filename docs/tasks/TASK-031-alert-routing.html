TASK-031 — Alerts: who is told what, when, and what happens if nobody answers

2026-09-14 · Phase 4 of the task & project portal plan · migration 0011 · core alerts.ts · a rewritten routing.ts · a channel-aware outbox · 10 routes · an inbox and an Alerts tab · 16 core tests + 6 route tests · browser-verified · 283 tests green

One-sentence summary: Every alert recipient is now a row somebody can read — the resolver, the raiser's manager, the department lead, the CEO, a task's watchers — chosen by a table lookup that records which rule picked them, delivered through an outbox that knows about channels, folded into one alert per problem however many times it is reported, and escalated up a ladder of rungs with timeouts instead of straight to the CEO forever.

2 · The design, and where it comes from

The decomposition is the one PagerDuty and Opsgenie converged on, because alerting really does have four independent parts and conflating any two of them is what makes alert systems unmaintainable.

DecisionReference
An escalation policy of ordered levels, each with a timeout, each naming targetsPagerDuty escalation policies (their own default timeout is 30 min)
Symbolic targets — "the manager of whoever raised it", not a uuid — resolved to people at fire timeJira notification schemes (reporter, assignee, watchers, role), so rules survive staff changes
A level with nobody available is skipped, not dropped: recorded with escalated_to NULL, and the next rung is tried in the same callPagerDuty's documented behaviour; the NULL row is what stops the sweep retrying the same empty rung forever
Alias de-duplication: a repeat of the same problem while the alert is open increments countOpsgenie alias-based dedup. This is the direct answer to the two identical escalation messages
Three states: triggered → acknowledged → resolved. Acknowledged ≠ resolvedStandard across both; "somebody is on it" and "it is fixed" are different questions
Per-person notification rules (channel, mode, delay), separate from who is toldPagerDuty per-user notification rules
An in-app notification store as the primary channel, with Telegram/email/push as delivery hintsWhat Jira, Asana, GitLab and Discourse do. It is also the channel that needs no external service at all — the foundation of the resilience proposal

The idempotency key is structural

`${event.type}:${entityId}:${employeeId}:${channel}`
  → "task.assigned:<assignment>:<person>:telegram"
  → "blocker.escalated:<blocker>#2:<person>:inapp"

Because the outbox key is UNIQUE, processing the same event twice enqueues nothing twice — per person, per channel, per escalation level. That is the idempotency requirement met by construction rather than by a check.

What we have not verified

  • Every timing in the ladder is assumed. Rung 1 immediately, rung 2 after 30 minutes, rung 3 after 60. FreshNow has not said what its real escalation timings are. They are rows in escalation_level, changeable without a deploy — but they are placeholders.
  • The SLA windows are the old assumed constants (15/60/240/1440 min), now in a table. Still assumed.
  • The ladder is degenerate in the seeded org, because every employee's manager is the CEO. The manager rung and the CEO rung resolve to the same person today. The mechanism is tested with a real manager in the test fixtures; the live org chart is not yet real.
  • No department lead exists, so the department_lead target type is exercised only by the resolver returning nobody. It is in the ladder's vocabulary but not on the default ladder.
  • Email and web push are not built — declaring a preference for them produces no row at all, by design. The channel column and the per-channel sender are ready; the senders are not written.
  • "digest" mode is accepted and honoured as "not now". No digest is ever assembled or sent. A person who chooses digest is choosing silence on that channel until digests exist. The UI does not offer it for this reason.
  • The bot does not show the inbox. Notifications reach Telegram as messages; the in-app inbox is dashboard-only.
  • Alias de-duplication needs a named asset. Without one, each report is its own alert — deliberately, because guessing that two free-text reports are "the same problem" would suppress a real one. The failure mode is one message too many, never one too few.
  • Dedup was not verified against a real repeat typed into the bot — only through the core function with two blockers naming the same asset.

Security, privacy and honesty

  • Every recipient is a table lookup. No model call decides who is told, whether to escalate, or when. The CLAUDE.md rule holds: the LLM never decides who or whether.
  • Every alert records why. alert.enqueued carries each recipient, their channel and the matched rule id; alert.no_recipient records the case worth knowing about — a rule that matched nobody, meaning somebody should have been told and was not.
  • RLS on every new table, default deny. A person reads their own inbox and their own rules; alerts are visible to whoever may see the person they are about; policy tables are readable configuration like routing_rule. Each of those has a test with a viewer who must see it and one who must not.
  • Nothing is sent on the CEO's behalf. The acknowledge keyboard is still an explicit tap, and the permission rule behind it is now one function shared by the bot and the dashboard rather than two.
  • No individual scoring. The alert data supports "how often was this problem reported" and "how quickly was it acknowledged". It is not, and must not become, a per-person responsiveness score.
  • One behaviour change worth stating plainly: the sweep now climbs the ladder, so blockers that were stuck at level 1 for days will escalate on the next sweep after this migration. On the live demo database five genuinely-overdue open blockers moved to level 2 the moment the worker restarted. That is the system doing what it was asked to do, but it is a visible change and not a silent one.
