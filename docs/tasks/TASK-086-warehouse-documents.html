One-sentence summary: the warehouse can now write down a delivery when it orders it, receive it at the door against what was expected — counted and weighed, with the rest left to come later or closed short, and every line a lot — send stock back to a supplier (never more than came), keep a least and a most for each item and get a suggestion of what to order, and read four reports (stock at the end of any day, every movement with a spreadsheet download, one item's whereabouts and lots, and each supplier's deliveries counted) — all on top of the existing stock ledger, so there is still one record of where stock is.
A bug found by looking at the screen, fixed before release: receiving 8 of 10 cartons with 150.5 kg against a note of 180 kg, the receive screen said "29.5 kg lighter than the note" — but 8 cartons should weigh 144 kg by that note, so they were heavier. The server had the same rule: it counted the two missing cartons once as cartons and again as weight, and would have told the Ops Head "150.5 of 180 kg". The scale is now held to the note's weight for what came (D285), everywhere the rule is used. The test that should have caught it had restated the code's rule instead of the business's (G232).

Why it was done this way

  • On top of the ledger, not beside it (D281). Televend's module, like Odoo's, keeps planned documents and stock moves together. Here the ledger (0032) already decides balances, counts and the day sheet; a second store of stock would be a second truth. A planned document only posts an ordinary ledger document when it is done, so everything built on the ledger keeps working.
  • Each line starts at what was expected (D282). At the door "everything came" must be one tap; the receiver changes only what differs. Less than expected asks the one question that matters — will the rest come? — as Odoo's backorder prompt does (VF103).
  • Weight against the note's weight for what came (D285). A note weighs everything expected; holding 8 cartons to the weight of 10 punishes a short delivery twice and hides a real light one. Catch-weight systems likewise set the tolerance per unit (VF104).
  • Suggestions, never orders (D284). CLAUDE.md: rules and lookups decide, people act. The suggestion counts what is already coming, so the same order is not suggested twice, and rounds up to whole packs as Odoo 19 does (VF102).
  • Counted, not scored (D286). The supplier report counts deliveries, lines and kilograms — about suppliers, never a person.
  • The door first (D287). The person receiving holds a phone: big buttons, amounts with − and +, one thing per sheet, and the expected delivery offered before a free one, so a delivery is not entered twice.

How it works

A delivery

  1. Expect a delivery: supplier, day, the delivery note's number, the truck's plate, the items with the note's kilograms (variety and best-before for oranges). "Save as draft" (not ordered yet) or "Save — expected". Nothing is in stock yet.
  2. On its day it appears on the Stock home under Expected at the door, and at the top of the free "Delivery in".
  3. Receive: each line starts at the expected amount; change what differs, type the scale's kilograms. The screen says "2 cartons short", and the note's weight for what came ("The note's weight for 8 cartons: 144 kg"; under it, "1.2 kg under …").
  4. Less than expected: "It will come later" makes a new expected delivery for the rest (a backorder, numbered, linked); "It will not come" closes it short.
  5. Done: the stock is in the warehouse through the ledger; each line is a lot IN-2026-0004-1; if it came short or a line is lighter than the note's weight for what came by more than 2 % (a placeholder), the Ops Head is told.

A return to the supplier

From a received delivery: Return some to …, a reason, how many (at most what came, less what already went back), kilograms, words. It is ready to go back; Send back takes it out of the warehouse's stock (a supplier_return) and tells the Ops Head. A return can also be made on its own from Deliveries. An approver can undo it like any stock document.

What to keep (reorder)

Example from the browser check: a rule for oranges in the warehouse — least 10, most 30, packs of 5. The warehouse held 6 cartons and 2 were still coming (the backorder): forecast 8, below 10, so the suggestion is enough to reach 30 in whole packs: 30 − 8 = 22, rounded up to 25. "Make a delivery draft" creates one draft per supplier; with it counted as coming, the list then says nothing to order. A rule for a person's stock suggests what to give them; "Give out" sends it as a handover they confirm.

Reports

  • Stock on a day: every move up to the end of that Dubai day, summed — warehouse, with people, being handed over.
  • Every movement: one row per item moved, newest first, with the document number; totals in and out of the warehouse (or a chosen person's stock) over the whole filter; Download as a spreadsheet (CSV) — up to 10,000 rows, UTF-8 with the mark Excel needs, and a cell starting with = + − @ written as text so no spreadsheet runs it.
  • One item: where it is now, the lots it came in, what moved it.
  • Suppliers: received, on time (of those with a day), lines complete, weight against the note for what came, lighter lines, returns, still to come — with the tolerance the server counted with.

Concepts and techniques

  • Ids made by the app: every create carries a UUID made when the sheet opens, so a double tap or a retry creates once; receiving the same document again with the same ledger id answers "done" instead of posting twice.
  • One transaction: the document turns done, the ledger posts, the lots and the backorder are written together or not at all.
  • Notices after the commit (through the outbox): who is told never decides whether stock moved.
  • Immutability by trigger, not by convention: a done document cannot be changed even by a stray SQL update.
  • Hidden while counting (D236): suggestions and reports would show the warehouse's figures to the person counting it.
  • CSV injection and the byte-order mark (G233).
  • Pro-rated tolerance: noteGramsFor and isLight, one rule in TypeScript and the same in SQL.

Research findings

  • Televend's Warehouse Management guide 1.1 (the user's attachment): an Odoo-style module — receipts drafted, marked to do, received against what was expected, a backorder for the rest, returns from done documents, minimum and maximum quantities. Used as the reference the user asked for; built stricter (weights, lots, one ledger, notices).
  • Odoo 19 reordering rules [verified, VF102]: the documentation change "multiple qty" (odoo/documentation#14853, merged 13 Nov 2025) — "Odoo now selects the multiple that exceeds the defined Max quantity, rather than stopping below it (as in v18)"; Max 20 with packs of 3 orders 21.
  • Odoo backorders [believed, VF103 — third-party guides and the Odoo forum]: Create Backorder (done + a new Ready receipt for the rest) or No Backorder (closed as it is).
  • Catch weight [believed, VF104 — search results only; learn.microsoft.com did not open from this container]: Dynamics 365 sets the weight tolerance per catch-weight unit, so the allowance grows with the units received — the same idea as D285.
