Why it was done this way

One ledger, moves only

The WhatsApp list is "copied forward, not counted" (VF74): the same numbers two days running, nothing about who took or brought back what. A balance column that people edit would repeat that. So nobody edits a number: every change is a move from one place to another, the balance is their sum kept by the database itself, and a mistake is put right by a reversal that stays visible next to the original. Because each move takes from one place exactly what it gives another, every item sums to zero over all places — a test checks it after 80 random postings.

Two people for every handover

"How many oranges you took and how many you brought back" is the CEO's daily question in the groups. A handover that one side records alone is the same message in a new place. Here the stock is in transit until the other side confirms, so a carton always has exactly one holder — and when the counts differ, the difference is visible to the approvers, not argued about later.

The count screen hides the figure — but the screen is not the control

Blind counting is recommended practice in warehouse guides (one vendor's guide calls it "the gold standard" — a vendor blog, not a study). The keeper here also runs the warehouse and can open the stock list, so the figure cannot really be hidden from them. The control is a second person for any difference beyond tolerance, spot checks by the Ops Head, and a record of who counted what and when (D228). The task does not pretend otherwise.

No stock is seeded

Seeding the WhatsApp numbers would start the ledger from figures known to be copied. The first count — approved, with the reason "opening stock" — sets it.

What was deliberately not built

Per-lot balances (no evidence the warehouse keeps deliveries apart; the delivery's variety and carton weight are kept on its lines), unit conversions, a second warehouse, counting a person's stock (they hand it back and the warehouse counts it), loading into machines as a place (TASK-061), reconciliation against Televend's cups (TASK-062), the phone app's offline queue (TASK-063).

How it works

Phone / browser makes the id→core: who may (capabilities), enough? (locked per item)→ stock_doc + lines + moves + audit, one transaction→trigger: balances (CHECK ≥ 0)→notices (best effort)→doorbell → screens refresh
hand-out   warehouse ──send──▶ in transit ──accept──▶ driver          (driver confirms)
             └────────────────── refuse / cancel / settle back ◀───┘
return     driver ──send──▶ in transit ──accept──▶ warehouse          (someone at the warehouse confirms)
less came  in transit ──write off (reason)──▶ count adjustments       (an approver who was not a party)
count      handed in → equal: closed · small: approval · big or moved: recount by someone else → approval
           decision: the count is right → adjustment doc (warehouse ↔ count adjustments) · the record is right → nothing moves

Day sheet, per item: start + in − given out + back − used − damaged ± counts = end — both sides summed from the same moves, so the line always adds up.

  • A real keeper or driver has not used it. The flows are tested and checked in a browser; nobody at the warehouse has counted or handed over stock with it yet.
  • The units of most items are assumptions (A-T60.2) — the keeper must confirm them before the first count; a unit is fixed after an item moves.
  • Photos on stock documents were not uploaded in the browser check (the upload API is TASK-058's, tested there); thumbnails in the movement detail were not seen with a real photo.
  • Notices on a real phone: the outbox rows are tested; a keeper's phone receiving "count what is left" has not been seen.
  • The reminder delays (30 minutes) and one warehouse are assumptions (A-T60.3, A-T60.4).
  • The demo reset's stock purge was not run against the live database.
  • Concurrency beyond two simultaneous hand-outs, and speed at a year's volume — unmeasured (the volume expected is hundreds of moves a day).
  • The review's skeptic pass did not run on 18 Oct (usage limit): the 17 findings were confirmed by the developer reading the code. On 20 Oct an independent review with skeptics checked those fixes (below). The wording on the take-back sheet is screen only, checked by the typecheck, not by a test.

  • The notice's new lines are a developer's draft, not FreshNow's or a lawyer's (notice 3.0 is still "draft").
