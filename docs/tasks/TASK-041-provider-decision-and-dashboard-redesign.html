TASK-041 — The provider decision, the cost model, and the dashboard redesign

2026-09-19 · no migration · api: one new route · dashboard: new shell, icon set, week chart, search · two reports · 376 tests green (from 375)

One-sentence summary: Answered four questions the company asked — which AI provider is lawful for real employee data, what it will cost at 100 assignments and 200 status updates a day, whether Telegram may be used at all, and whether the dashboard can look like a modern operations product — and the last one was built, not described.

1 · The provider verdict

Groq, OpenRouter and NVIDIA are out for production employee data; Core42 Compass is in, with OpenAI's UAE endpoint second. Four reasons, each tied to an article of the law rather than to a preference: no processor contract is possible on self-serve terms (Art. 7(5), Art. 8(1)); they process outside the UAE with no adequacy route available while the Executive Regulations are unissued (Art. 22); the consent employees gave never mentioned an AI service abroad; and the fallback chain silently sends the same text to a second and third US company when the first rate-limits. The reasoning, with the law quoted, is in the report.

The practical point: Core42 hosts the same model we already run (openai/gpt-oss-120b), so this is a base URL, a key, and LLM_PROVIDER_ORDER=core42 with the US providers removed — then re-run the contract tests and the benchmark. Not a rewrite.

3 · The Telegram verdict

Lawful, conditionally. The TDRA regulates VoIP, not text — our bot never calls. The PDPL does not ban the transfer; it permits it on express consent (Art. 23(1)(b)). The condition is the one thing we do not yet have: a notice that says Telegram stores messages outside the UAE. Until that is re-consented, every message is a transfer with no ground we can point at. The open question a lawyer must settle is whether a regulator would treat Telegram as an appointed processor (which would need a contract Telegram does not offer) or as an independent service like an email provider — my reading is the latter, and I have said so as a reading, not a finding.

4 · The dashboard redesign

The reference screenshot showed a product with a persistent sidebar, a search bar, coloured status cards and a chart. Ours had a text-emoji nav strip and six flat tiles. What was built:

PieceWhat it doesWhy that way
Sidebar shell
App.tsx		 Persistent 248px rail: gradient logo mark, a Tasks/Projects switch, grouped navigation with icons and live counts, an active-item gradient bar, and the signed-in person with their role and Sign out at the foot. Below 1024px it collapses to the icon grid that was already there. The portal switch and the section list were competing for the header; moving them out gives the content the full width and makes "where am I" answerable at a glance.
Icon set
components/icons.tsx		 24 inline line icons, 1.75 stroke, currentColor, aria-hidden. No dependency, and no emoji — emoji render differently per platform and are read aloud by screen readers. Every icon sits beside a text label, so it never carries meaning alone.
Status cards Six cards with a coloured left edge, an icon badge, the number, and the definition underneath ("critical or high problems nobody has resolved"). A "View →" affordance appears on hover and keyboard focus. The old tiles showed a number and a two-word label; the definition is what stops a number being misread. Colour is status — red act now, amber watch, green done, blue information — never decoration.
Last-seven-days chart
components/week-chart.tsx		 Stacked columns, completed / pending / blocked. Legend, one direct label (the day's total on the cap), tooltip on hover and keyboard focus, a Table toggle showing the same numbers as text, and a click that opens that day. Per the data-viz rules: states wear status colours (not a categorical rainbow), ≤24px columns, a 2px surface gap between segments, 4px rounded cap only on the top segment, hairline gridlines, and nothing reachable by colour alone.
Search
components/search.tsx		 One box over people, open tasks, open blockers and assignments, with ↑↓/Enter/Esc, ARIA combobox roles, and a jump to whatever shows the result. Client-side over data already loaded — which means the results are exactly what this viewer may see, with no new endpoint to get RLS wrong in.
Needs a human panel Urgent open problems and updates the parser could not read, in one list, each a button to the tab that handles it. Empty state says why it is empty. These were previously two numbers on two tiles with no way to see which items they counted.
GET /dashboard/week
api/routes/dashboard.ts		 Seven company days, three counts each, computed in SQL under the viewer's row-level rules. Days with nothing reported still appear. The engine computes, the browser draws — the chart cannot show a number the viewer may not see, and the browser never adds anything up. Day boundaries are Dubai midnight, not UTC.

What we have not verified

  • Nobody has signed up to Core42. The price, the model id and the UAE-jurisdiction claim are from their pages; that their API accepts our prompts unchanged is an expectation the contract tests will settle, not a finding. The same goes for OpenAI's UAE endpoint, which needs approval we have not requested.
  • The cost model's assumptions are mine, not the company's: 40 employees, 10 questions a day, 20% of updates as 30-second voice notes, 5 tasks per PDF. The arithmetic is linear — change a number, scale the line.
  • Token counts come from mostly English and transliterated Hinglish. Native Devanagari or Malayalam script tokenises 2–3× worse; the worst case doubles the status-update line and is still cents.
  • The chart has only ever been seen with demo data — at most 4 reports in a day. A day with 200 updates has not been rendered, so column scaling at real volume is untested (the y-axis rounds to a nice max, which should hold, but "should" is the word).
  • The first light-theme screenshot was a stale frame. Setting data-theme directly and shooting 400ms later produced a dark image although the assertion was true. Re-run through the app's own theme button with a longer wait: the page really is light, the chart really does use the light palette, and the contrast numbers above were sampled from that render. Worth remembering that a passing assertion and a screenshot can disagree — the screenshot lags.
  • Dark-mode contrast was computed from the tokens, not sampled from a render, unlike light.
  • No colour-blind person has looked at this. The palette passes the simulation-based checks; that is a model of CVD, not a user test.
  • The Telegram-as-processor question is a reading, not an opinion from counsel.
