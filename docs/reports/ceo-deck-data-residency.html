The answer in one slide

Four questions, four answers

1 · Must personal data stay in the UAE?

Not as a general rule. The federal law (Decree-Law 45/2021) regulates transfer abroad; it does not forbid it. Strict in-country rules exist for health, banking/payments and government data — none of which is FreshNow's employee task data. [verified — the law's text, slide 3]

The catch: the rules that would make transfers straightforward have not been issued as of March 2026, so today companies rely on consent and contracts. [believed]

2 · Does our data leave the country today?

Yes, four ways — Telegram (unavoidable), three US AI companies (avoidable), call metadata to Ireland (avoidable), and the planned Hostinger server, which has no UAE data centre at all. [verified — slides 5–6]

3 · Why not keep Groq / OpenRouter?

No processor contract on self-serve terms (Art. 7, 8); processing abroad with no adequacy route (Art. 22); a consent notice that never mentioned it; and a fallback chain that silently sends the same text to a second US company. [verified — slide 8]

4 · What instead, and what does it cost?

Core42 (a G42 company) runs the exact model we use today under UAE jurisdiction. A full day — 100 assignments, 200 status updates, 40 end-of-day summaries, 10 questions — costs about $0.12; with voice notes about $7 a month. [verified prices · measured tokens — slides 9–12]

What the law says

Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 — the articles that decide this

It applies to us [verified]

Art. 2(1)(b): applies to "any Controller or Processor located in the State who carries out the activities of Processing Personal Data of Data Subjects inside or outside the State."

Employment is a lawful basis beyond consent [verified]

Art. 4(8): processing "necessary for the Controller or Data Subject to fulfill his/her obligations … in the field of employment"; Art. 4(9): "necessary to perform a contract".

We must appoint processors under contract [verified]

Art. 7(5): "appoint a Processor who provides sufficient guarantees"; Art. 8(1): processing "in accordance with … the contracts and agreements concluded between them".

Transfer abroad — the two articles everything turns on [verified]

Art. 22: transfer allowed "in the following cases approved by the Office" — a country with adequate data-protection law, or a bilateral agreement.
Art. 23(1): otherwise, allowed under (a) a contract imposing this law's controls, (b) "the express Consent of the Data Subject", (c) judicial claims, (d) "necessary to enter into or execute a contract", (e) judicial cooperation, (f) public interest.

Keep only as long as needed [verified]

Art. 5(7): "Personal Data may not be kept after fulfilling the purpose of Processing" unless anonymised.

Source: English translation of the Decree-Law, LexisNexis Middle East (PDF); official Arabic listing uaelegislation.gov.ae.

Sector rules: Clifford Chance, Data Transfers in the UAE and KSA (Mar 2025): "the UAE PDPL itself does not impose a general data localisation requirement"; PwC, Healthcare data protection in the UAE. [believed]

The complication: the Executive Regulations are still missing

Art. 28 required them within six months of the law (i.e. by early 2022). They set the adequacy list, the contract templates and the transfer procedure.

Chambers, Data Protection & Privacy 2026 — UAE (10 Mar 2026): "The Implementing Regulations … have yet to be issued" and "the Data Office has not yet become fully operational."

What that means in practice: the "adequate country" route does not exist yet; the usable grounds are express consent and contracts. And Art. 29 gives a six-month regularisation window that starts the day the Regulations appear — so the clock has not started, and will.

[uncertain] I could not find a primary source showing issuance between March and September 2026. One search result claimed a 2026 publication; the page it cited said "in progress". Please have counsel check the Official Gazette.

Why not the Hostinger VPS

Hostinger has no data centre in the UAE or anywhere in the Middle East

Hostinger's own list of VPS locations [verified — their support page]

"Europe: France, Germany, Lithuania, and the United Kingdom. Asia: India, Indonesia, and Malaysia. North America: USA (Phoenix and Boston). South America: Brazil."

Choosing the KVM 8 plan means the entire employee database — every name, every status, every word typed — lives permanently in one of those countries.

Under the PDPL that is a continuous cross-border transfer of the whole dataset (Art. 22–23), with no adequacy decision to rely on, resting entirely on consent that today's notice does not even describe.

Where are Hostinger servers located? · KVM 8 = 8 vCPU, 32 GB, 400 GB NVMe at $29.99/mo introductory, $49.99/mo on renewal [believed — 2026 pricing round-ups]

Why this is the first thing to change

  • It is the largest transfer by far. The AI calls carry one message at a time; the database carries everything, forever.
  • It is the easiest to fix. The software is a Docker Compose stack; it runs identically on any Linux box. Nothing in the code changes — only where it runs.
  • It has not happened yet. Today the database is on a laptop in Dubai. Choosing the right host before go-live avoids a migration later.
  • It is the one decision that also unlocks the rest: a UAE host with a domain gives us HTTPS, which web push, inbound email and the Telegram webhook all need.
Decision needed: choose a host with a UAE region (next slide). Prices shown are like-for-like where possible and honest where not.

Why not Groq, OpenRouter or NVIDIA API keys

Four reasons — each tied to an article, not a preference

1 · No contract is possible on self-serve terms

Art. 7(5) requires an appointed processor "who provides sufficient guarantees"; Art. 8(1) requires processing under "contracts and agreements concluded between them". A pay-as-you-go API key is not that. Groq's own privacy policy says customer data is governed by a separate Services Agreement and DPA that a self-serve key does not give you. [verified — Groq policy, 12 Nov 2025]

2 · They process outside the UAE, with no adequacy route

Groq's data centres: US, Canada, Saudi Arabia, Finland, Australia — no UAE, and no documented way to pin a region. NVIDIA and OpenRouter are US. With the Executive Regulations unissued, Art. 22 cannot be used; only consent or contract (Art. 23) remain. [verified — Groq newsroom]

3 · Our consent does not cover it

The notice employees agreed to says updates are "visible to you and the CEO" — nothing about an AI service abroad, nothing about voice notes being transcribed abroad. Art. 6 requires consent to be specific. [verified — the notice text in our code]

4 · The fallback chain leaks silently

Our configuration was groq → openrouter → nvidia. On 19 September this actually happened: Groq timed out at 25 s on every call and OpenRouter absorbed the traffic — visible in our logs as groq ok=false 25,058 ms followed by openrouter ok=true. Nobody was told, and employee text reached a second US company. Good engineering; wrong residency. [verified — our llm_call table]

The fix is not "build our own AI". The model we use — openai/gpt-oss-120b — needs ~61 GB of memory just for its weights; our server has 32 GB and no GPU. Training a model on company data is the wrong tool for these tasks and would need data we do not have. The fix is to run the same model at a provider in the UAE, under a contract, with less personal data in each request — next slide. [verified — llama.cpp's gpt-oss guide: 61.0 GB]

In plain terms: at this volume the AI bill is a rounding error next to one server. The decision is about where the words are read and under what contract, not about money.

What would move these numbers

  • Script. Hindi or Malayalam typed in native script tokenises 2–3× worse than the Latin-script Hinglish in our logs. Worst case doubles the status line — still cents.
  • Head-count. Summaries scale with people, not updates: 100 employees add about $0.01 a day.
  • Reasoning setting. gpt-oss-120b is a reasoning model; at a high setting its output tokens (the dear side) can multiply. Our measured 340-token outputs are at the default.
  • Retries. One retry on a malformed answer; measured failure rates are low. Budget 10% headroom.
  • Assumptions you own: 40 employees · 10 questions a day · 20% voice at 30 s · 5 tasks per PDF. Change any of them and the arithmetic is linear.

Telegram

Can we use Telegram at all? Yes — lawfully, on one condition

RuleWhat it saysOur position
TDRA — is the app allowed?VoIP is a licensed telecom service; unlicensed calling is what is restricted. Text and files are not. [verified — TDRA FAQ]Allowed. The bot never makes calls.
PDPL Art. 22–23 — data stored abroadTelegram stores cloud chats in data centres worldwide, none in the UAE, and offers no business contract. Transfer is permitted with "the express Consent of the Data Subject" (Art. 23(1)(b)). [verified]Lawful only after the notice names Telegram and staff consent again. Today's notice does not.
Is Telegram our "processor"?A processor acts on our instructions under contract; Telegram is a service the employee also uses privately.My reading: an independent service, like an email provider — counsel should confirm.
Employee monitoring (Labour Law 33/2021)Must be disclosed, proportionate, purpose-limited. [believed]The bot is a self-report channel: no location, no scoring, no reading of private chats.

So, in practice

  • Keep Telegram as the primary channel — especially for drivers and field staff: already installed, no password, one tap, and the Acknowledge tap is proof a human read it.
  • Re-issue the consent notice so it names Telegram, the AI provider and where each runs; ask everyone again. The system already versions and hashes the notice.
  • Keep the database as the only record — already true; Telegram carries messages, it stores nothing we rely on.
  • Have an in-country channel ready — the dashboard inbox and web push are built and switched off, so a rule change or a ban is a switch, not a rebuild.

What is not verified

Where this briefing could be wrong, stated plainly

  • Whether the Executive Regulations have been issued since March 2026. Chambers says no; no primary source found either way. Counsel should check the Official Gazette.
  • Whether Daily Vending LLC is a mainland or free-zone company. Assumed mainland; DIFC/ADGM would change the regime.
  • Whether a regulator would treat Telegram as a processor needing a contract it does not offer. A reading, not counsel's opinion.
  • Hyperscaler prices are from third-party trackers (dated 18–19 Sep 2026) and exclude disk, bandwidth and any reserved-instance discount. Confirm on the official calculators before signing.
  • Core42's logging and retention terms were not found on the pages read. Get the DPA in writing before sending real employee text. Their API has not yet been tested with our prompts.
  • OpenAI's 10% uplift and UAE eligibility come from their pricing page and developer docs; the UAE endpoint "requires additional approval" we have not requested.
  • The cost model's assumptions are mine: 40 employees, 10 questions, 20% voice at 30 s, 5 tasks per PDF. Linear — change a number, the line scales.
  • Token counts are from mostly English and Latin-script Hinglish. Native Devanagari or Malayalam tokenises 2–3× worse.
  • Three search-engine summaries were wrong on the primary page ("regulations issued in 2026", "Google has a UAE region", "Claude runs in-region in the UAE") and were corrected here — which is why every number carries a marker and a link.
  • No lawyer has reviewed this. It is a technical reading built to be checked quickly, and it should be.
