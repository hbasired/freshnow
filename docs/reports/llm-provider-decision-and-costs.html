Which AI provider, what it costs, and the Telegram verdict

Prepared 2026-09-19 for FreshNow · follows UAE data residency, the PDPL, and where our AI should run and Telegram alternatives — resilience and data residency · prices read from the vendors' own pages today · token counts measured from our own call log

How to read this. Not legal advice — a technical reading of the law's text and of what our system does, for a UAE lawyer to confirm. [verified] = primary source or our own data; [believed] = reputable secondary sources; [assumed] = a number I had to pick, stated so you can change it.

1 · Groq / OpenRouter, or a UAE-hosted provider? — the verdict

Do not use Groq or OpenRouter (or NVIDIA) for real employee data in production. Use Core42 Compass, with OpenAI's UAE endpoint as the second option. Keep the Groq/OpenRouter keys for development against synthetic (DEMO) data only.

I re-read the relevant articles of Federal Decree-Law 45/2021 and the vendor documentation before writing this. Four reasons, in order of weight:

  1. No processor contract is possible on those terms. Art. 7(5) requires the controller to "appoint a Processor who provides sufficient guarantees", and Art. 8(1) requires processing "in accordance with the instructions of the Controller and the contracts and agreements concluded between them" [verified]. Groq, OpenRouter and NVIDIA's self-serve APIs are consumer-grade terms; Groq's privacy policy says customer data is governed by a separate Services Agreement and DPA that a self-serve key does not give you [verified — Groq privacy policy, effective 12 Nov 2025]. Core42 and OpenAI both offer business terms and DPAs.
  2. They process outside the UAE with no route under Art. 22. Groq runs in the US, Canada, Saudi Arabia, Finland and Australia — no UAE, and no way I could find to pin a region; NVIDIA and OpenRouter are US [verified]. The adequacy route (Art. 22) does not exist yet because the Executive Regulations have not been issued (as of March 2026) [believed — Chambers 2026], so the only grounds left are express consent (Art. 23(1)(b)) or a contract importing the law's controls (Art. 23(1)(a)) — the second of which brings us back to reason 1.
  3. Our consent does not cover it. The notice employees agreed to says nothing about an AI service abroad [verified — quoted in the residency report §5]. Until a new notice is consented, every Groq call with a real person's words is a transfer with no lawful ground we can point to.
  4. The fallback chain makes it worse silently. LLM_PROVIDER_ORDER=groq,openrouter,nvidia means a rate-limit at one US company routes the same employee text to a second and a third, with no notice to anyone. A residency posture cannot have a fallback that leaves the country.

What "use Core42" means in practice — one configuration change and one code change, then re-run the existing contract tests and the model benchmark:

  • Sign up to Core42 Compass, get the DPA in writing, and put its OpenAI-compatible base URL and key in .env as a new provider entry. The model is the same openai/gpt-oss-120b we run today, so prompts and the extraction contract tests should pass unchanged — verify, do not assume [Core42's API format is described as "Chat Completion"; I have not tested it].
  • Set LLM_PROVIDER_ORDER=core42 and remove the US providers from the fallback list. When Core42 is down or rate-limited, the wrapper's existing degradation applies: the update is stored verbatim, flagged needs a human, and a person is alerted — nothing is lost and nothing leaves the country.
  • Voice: point transcription at Azure UAE North's Whisper (Standard deployment — in-region [verified — Microsoft's region table]) or Core42's GPT-4o Transcribe (preview); until then, turn voice off. Groq Whisper stays off.
  • Optionally add OpenAI's UAE endpoint (ae.api.openai.com, requires approval) as a second in-country provider for the question box, where a stronger model helps. Its cheap eligible model is gpt-5.6-luna.

Honest note on the past: during development, 250 chat calls and one voice note went to Groq, 54 to NVIDIA, 17 to OpenRouter. Most carried DEMO (synthetic) text; the one real registered person's updates went through Groq. That is history the audit log records; the fix is forward-looking.

With Core42 running the same model we use now, a full day of this volume costs about 12 cents in chat tokens and another 12 cents in voice — roughly $7–8 a month, under $100 a year, in scenario D. OpenAI's gpt-5.6-luna in the UAE is about 2.5× that — still under $10/month. The "flagship" OpenAI models (5.2, 5.5) are 25–60× dearer and buy nothing for extraction and routing; if you ever want them, use them for the CEO's question box only. The channel mix barely matters: assignments are the cheap operation, and the dashboard/button path is free. Status updates dominate because each carries two model calls. The current LLM_DAILY_BUDGET_USD=2 cap covers every Core42 and luna scenario with room to spare.

What would change these numbers

  • Script. Hindi and Malayalam typed in native script tokenise 2–3× worse than Latin transliteration; our measured averages are mostly transliterated Hinglish and English. Worst case, double the status-update line.
  • Employee count. EOD narratives scale with people, not updates: 100 employees add ~$0.01/day on Core42.
  • Retries. The wrapper retries once on a bad JSON; measured failure rates are low, but budget 10% headroom.
  • Reasoning effort. gpt-oss-120b is a reasoning model; if it is run at a high reasoning setting, output tokens (the expensive side) can multiply several-fold. Our measured 340-token outputs are at the default.

3 · Telegram — can we use it, or is it a violation?

Using Telegram for this is not a violation of UAE law as written, provided the notice says what it does and the person agrees. It is a permitted cross-border transfer on the consent ground, not a prohibited one — and it must be treated as a transfer, not ignored.

RuleWhat it saysOur position
TDRA — is the app itself allowed?VoIP is a licensed telecom service; unlicensed voice/video calling is the restricted feature. Text, buttons and files are not [verified — TDRA FAQ]Allowed. The bot never uses calls.
PDPL Art. 2 — does the law apply?Yes: a controller in the State processing data of subjects in the State [verified]Applies to every message.
PDPL Art. 22–23 — data stored abroadTelegram stores cloud chats in data centres worldwide, none in the UAE, and offers no business contract [verified — Telegram privacy policy]. Transfer without adequacy is permitted with "the express Consent of the Data Subject to transfer his/her Personal Data outside the State" (Art. 23(1)(b)) [verified]Lawful only after the notice names Telegram and the person consents to it. Today's notice does not — fix it first (draft in the residency report §5).
PDPL Art. 7(5) / 8 — is Telegram our processor?A processor acts on the controller's instructions under a contract. Telegram is a communications service the employee also uses privately; the company cannot instruct it or contract with itMy reading: Telegram is an independent service, like an email provider, not an appointed processor — a lawyer should confirm. If the regulator treats every channel as a processor needing a contract, Telegram fails that test and the in-app channel or WhatsApp Business (UAE storage) replaces it.
Sector localisation (health, banking, government)Strict in-country rules, not applicable to operational task data [believed]Does not apply — and keep it that way: never solicit health details in a status update.
Employee monitoring (Labour Law 33/2021, PDPL Art. 5)Monitoring must be disclosed, proportionate, purpose-limited [believed]The bot is a self-report channel: no location, no scoring, no sentiment, no reading of private chats. Disclosed in the notice.
Art. 6(2) — withdrawalConsent can be withdrawn at any timeBuilt: /withdraw disables the account; the CEO's erase action anonymises.
Art. 28–29 — Executive RegulationsMay set "controls and requirements" for the Art. 23 cases; six-month regularisation once issuedReview the Telegram position the day they are published.

So: keep Telegram as the primary channel, re-consent everyone with a notice that names it, keep Postgres as the only record (already true), and build the in-country in-app channel so that a change in the rules — or a Telegram ban — is a switch, not a rebuild.

4 · What I could not verify

  • Whether the Executive Regulations have been issued since March 2026 (Chambers says no; no primary source found either way).
  • Core42's logging/retention terms and its transcription price — not on the pages I read; get the DPA.
  • That Core42's API accepts our prompts unchanged — the contract tests will tell.
  • Whether a UAE regulator would treat Telegram as a processor requiring a contract — a lawyer's call.
  • The 40-employee, 10-question, 20%-voice and 5-tasks-per-PDF assumptions — change them in the table and the arithmetic is linear.
